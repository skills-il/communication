# Equal Employment Opportunities Law 1988: CV Compliance Checklist

The Employment (Equal Opportunities) Law 5748-1988 prohibits Israeli employers from **discriminating** in hiring based on specific protected characteristics.

**The Law does not, as a general rule, make asking the question an offence.** Getting this right matters, because the skill will be quoted. Two provisions do the real work:

- **Section 9(ג)**: in a claim under section 2, once it is proved the employer demanded such information from the employee or applicant, directly or indirectly, the burden of proof shifts to the employer to show it did not discriminate.
- **Section 15(ד)**: in a criminal proceeding under section 2, the same demand raises a rebuttable presumption that the accused breached the section.

**Section 21 limits all of the above.** Section 21(ג) disapplies the whole Law, except section 7, to a person employing fewer than six employees, and the count includes manpower-contractor workers placed with that person. Below that threshold none of section 2, section 2א or the sections 9(ג) and 15(ד) machinery applies at all. Section 21(ד) separately disapplies 9(ג) and 15(ד) where the information was demanded in order to comply with Commission directives under sections 18י and 18יא, with a statutory proper-representation or affirmative-action duty, or with an equality-promotion programme the employer operates or a government ministry approved or funds.

So the practical rule ("keep this off the CV") is right, but the reason is evidential, not a ban on questions, and it is a reason that runs out entirely at a very small employer. Volunteering the data on a CV hands the employer information it had every reason not to hold, and forfeits the candidate's later evidential advantage.

## Protected Characteristics (Do Not Include)

| Characteristic | Legal Basis | CV Action |
|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| Age | Section 2(a) | Omit date of birth, graduation year for older candidates |
| Gender | Section 2(a) | Omit gender identifier, use neutral pronouns where possible |
| Sexual orientation | Section 2(a) | Omit any reference |
| Personal / marital status | Section 2(a) | Omit marital status |
| Pregnancy | Section 2(a) | Omit pregnancy or family planning info |
| Fertility treatments | Section 2(a). A distinct ground from IVF | Omit |
| IVF treatments (הפריה חוץ-גופית) | Section 2(a). Enumerated separately from fertility treatments | Omit |
| Parenthood | Section 2(a) | Omit number of children, parental leave status |
| Race | Section 2(a) | Omit race / ethnicity |
| Religion | Section 2(a) | Omit religious affiliation (exception: religious role at religious institution) |
| Nationality / origin | Section 2(a) | Omit country of origin (exception: when work permit status is job-relevant) |
| Country of origin | Section 2(a) | Omit |
| Worldview (השקפה) | Section 2(a). A separate ground from party affiliation, and read broadly | Omit |
| Political party (מפלגה) | Section 2(a) | Omit political affiliation, activism |
| Place of residence | Section 2(a) | City only is fine for commute signaling. No street address, no neighborhood that could signal demographic |
| Reserve military service | Section 2(a). Also covers the reserve service of a spouse or a child's other parent, and covers frequency and duration | Omit frequency or specific reserve deployment schedule |
| Military profile (פרופיל צבאי) | **Section 2א**, a prohibition on the employer *demanding* it, or using it in any of the matters listed in section 2(א)(1) to (6), and on penalising a refusal to supply it. Shin Bet and Mossad as employers are carved out, and section 21(ג) disapplies it below six employees | Never include the profile number. Unit, role, rank and dates are fine |
| Criminal record | Criminal Information and Rehabilitation of Offenders Law, 5779-2019 (חוק המידע הפלילי ותקנת השבים). It controls who may receive criminal information; where an employer may receive it at all, the route runs through the police with the candidate's prior written consent | Never add a "no criminal record" line |

## Practical Checklist Before Submitting a CV

Run through this list before sending any Israeli CV:

- [ ] No photograph on the English version
- [ ] No date of birth anywhere
- [ ] No marital status or family status
- [ ] No number of children
- [ ] No religious affiliation (unless the role is at a religious institution)
- [ ] No political affiliation or activism
- [ ] No ID number (Teudat Zehut)
- [ ] No home address (city only)
- [ ] No "expected salary" line (salary is negotiated, not pre-committed)
- [ ] No reference list ("References available upon request" is also noise; remove it)
- [ ] Graduation year is acceptable if you are under ~35; older candidates may omit year and list only degree + institution

## When Photos Are Allowed

Photos are NEVER required in Israel and should always be omitted from the English CV. For Hebrew CVs, there are narrow exceptions:

| Role type | Photo allowed? |
|-----------|----------------|
| Tech, engineering, finance, legal, medicine, academia | No |
| Customer-facing corporate (receptionist, sales) | Discouraged but common |
| Acting, modeling | Commonly accepted where appearance is genuinely part of the role |
| Hospitality, cabin crew | Often requested in practice, but appearance requirements here are contested rather than an established carve-out. Do not present it to the user as settled |

Even where "allowed", the photo invites unconscious bias. If the user does not explicitly request it, leave it out.

## Bona Fide Occupational Qualification (BFOQ)

Section 2(c) of the law allows differential treatment "necessitated by the character or nature of the assignment or post" (essentially a BFOQ carve-out). Example: a dance academy hiring a ballerina can consider physical ability; a church hiring a pastor can consider religion. These exceptions are narrow and almost never apply to tech, office, or service roles.

Section 2(ג) is a defence available to an employer on a job-by-job basis, not a licence to collect personal data. Even for a role where appearance is genuinely part of the job, the skill should include a photo, age or gender only if the user explicitly asks. Note that section 2א (military profile) has no equivalent carve-out outside the Shin Bet and the Mossad.

## What the Employer Should Also Not Ask

This is for the candidate's awareness during interviews. There is no general statutory ban on the questions below, so an interviewer asking one is not automatically committing an offence. What matters is that asking them is legally costly for the employer: under section 9(ג) it shifts the burden of proof, and under section 15(ד) it raises a presumption in a criminal case. Subject, in both cases, to the section 21 limits above. A candidate is under no obligation to answer, and a refusal cannot lawfully be held against them on a protected ground.

The one question below that the statute prohibits outright is the military profile (section 2א), subject to the section 21 limits above and to its confinement to the matters in section 2(א)(1) to (6).

- "How old are you?"
- "Are you married?"
- "Do you plan to have children?"
- "What religion are you?"
- "Where are your parents from?"
- "Are you pregnant?"
- "What is your sexual orientation?"
- "What are your political views?"
- "When were you in reserves last?"
- "What is your military profile?" (section 2א, the employer may not demand this at all)

## Enforcement

The Equal Employment Opportunities Commission (נציבות שוויון הזדמנויות בעבודה), established under section 18א, handles **civil** enforcement and sits in the Ministry of Economy and Industry. (Section 1 of the Law still defines "the Minister" as the Minister of Labour and Social Affairs; that definition is historical, the unit today is under Economy.) Criminal enforcement of labour law runs through the Ministry of Economy's regulation and enforcement administration.

Remedies sit in **section 10**: the Labour Court has exclusive civil jurisdiction and may award damages **even where no pecuniary loss was caused**, and may grant injunctive relief. Do not quote the NIS 120,000 figure as a general discrimination cap: section 10(א)(1) applies that ceiling only to claims under section 7, which covers harm arising from sexual harassment and from a complaint about it, and it is index-linked. The general protection for a complainant is section 6, and it carries no such ceiling.
