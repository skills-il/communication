# Changelog

## 1.3.0 - 2026-08-24

Three corrections and two additions, all consumer-money relevant.

Exit fees: the skill told users that commitment contracts carry early-termination fees and cited a garbled "Consumer Protection Law 1981, up to 12 months remaining" rule that does not exist. Under Communications Law sections 51A and 51D a household subscriber pays nothing for cancelling; only outstanding device instalments and accrued debts survive, and section 51B bars accelerating them. The repealed 8 percent formula that Israeli consumer sites still republish is named as repealed. New Step 10 sets this out, and the internet section no longer contradicts it.

Number portability: "1 to 3 business days" was wrong. The Ministry of Communications numbering plan caps the switch at half an hour for a mobile number, one hour for a fixed line and three hours for a number inside a block, with a one-business-day statutory bound. Added the three facts that actually cost people their numbers: never cancel the outgoing line first, a completed port ends the outgoing service but not the debt, and a debt or live commitment is not a lawful ground to refuse a port.

Operator landscape: removed You Mobile (merged into Pelephone in 2015) and "Hashikma (YMax)" (Hashikma is Rami Levy's licensed entity, not a separate brand). Added wecom, Walla Mobile, Expon and Neptucom with their host networks, corrected 019 to Partner, noted Golan is Cellcom-owned, and that Partner is absorbing the 012 Mobile brand. Recorded that Partner and HOT Mobile share one radio network and that HOT Mobile's sale was cleared by the Competition Authority on 5 August 2026.

Added: the wholesale-market split between infrastructure and ISP for home internet, roaming protections (data roaming blocked by default, blocking on request within one business day, a subscriber-set spend cap), the ban on premium services to a mobile destination, the section 51C ban on carrier-locked handsets, and the online-cancellation link and six-minute human-answer rules.

## 1.2.2 - 2026-08-12

The 2G/3G wind-down is staged and not complete. The skill said Israel finished the shutdown in January 2026; the operators vacated the first-GHz bands on 1 February 2026 and the legacy networks continue to run on other frequencies, with emergency dialling from old handsets guaranteed to the end of 2028. Users with an alarm panel or elevator emergency line were being told their equipment may already be disconnected when it is not.
