# Israeli Business Calendar

Key dates, deadlines, and seasonal patterns for running a business or freelance practice in Israel. Use this as a planning reference when scheduling projects, setting deadlines, or preparing for slow periods.

---

## Recurring Monthly Deadlines

These deadlines repeat every month or every two months throughout the year.

| Date | Obligation | Who It Applies To |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| 15th of each month, or the 19th at 18:30 when filing online | VAT (מע"מ) payment and reporting | Businesses on monthly VAT cycle (turnover above threshold) |
| Per the payment voucher schedule sent by Bituach Leumi (standing-order bank debits are taken on the 22nd, for the previous month) | Bituach Leumi (ביטוח לאומי) contributions | Self-employed (עצמאים) |
| 15th of each month, or the 19th at 18:30 when paying online | Income tax advance (מקדמת מס הכנסה) | Self-employed with advance payment obligation. Bi-monthly only with an approval recorded in פנקס המקדמות |
| 15th of every odd month, or the 19th at 18:30 online | VAT reporting - bi-monthly cycle | Businesses on bi-monthly VAT cycle (annual turnover up to ₪1,775,000 in 2026; ₪1,805,000 from 1 Jan 2027) |
| 31 January | Annual turnover declaration | עוסק פטור, for the previous calendar year |

**Standard VAT rate (2026): 18%** (raised from 17% on Jan 1, 2025).

**חשבוניות ישראל / Allocation Number regime (חוק חשבוניות ישראל):** Tax-deductible business invoices above a threshold must include an Allocation Number (מספר הקצאה) generated in real time by the Tax Authority, or the buyer cannot claim the VAT input.

| Effective | Threshold (ex-VAT) |
|-----------|--------------------|
| Jan 1, 2026 | ₪10,000 per invoice |
| Jun 1, 2026 | ₪5,000 per invoice |

This affects every Israeli freelancer issuing invoices to businesses, and every business processing supplier invoices. Most accounting software (Green Invoice, iCount, Hashavshevet) handles allocation requests automatically once the integration is set up.

**VAT reporting periods (bi-monthly):**

| Reporting Period | Report Due By |
|-----------------|---------------|
| January-February | March 15 |
| March-April | May 15 |
| May-June | July 15 |
| July-August | September 15 |
| September-October | November 15 |
| November-December | January 15 (following year) |

Notes on the deadline:
- When the 15th falls on the business owner's weekly rest day, reporting and payment shift to the next business day.
- Filing online through the Tax Authority site extends both VAT and מקדמות to the **19th at 18:30**. This is the deadline that actually binds most freelancers.
- **Deadlines move in emergencies.** The Tax Authority defers filing and payment dates during wars and security operations. In 2026 the February monthly report and the Jan-Feb bi-monthly report were both deferred to 26.03.2026, and March reports filed by 29.04.2026 drew no penalty, linkage or interest. Check the Tax Authority's announcements for the current period rather than assuming the standard date. Reserve-duty service can earn an individual extension.

Always verify current dates with the Tax Authority (www.gov.il/taxes).

## Annual Tax Deadlines

These change slightly each year. Treat these as approximate; confirm current year dates with your accountant.

| Obligation | Approximate Period | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| Annual income tax return (דוח שנתי) - self-employed | April-May | With accountant extension: often pushed to June-July |
| Annual income tax return - employees with additional income | April-May | - |
| Employer annual reports (102 forms) | February | Report on employee wages from previous year |
| Pension contributions reconciliation | Quarterly | Varies by pension fund |

## Jewish Holiday Calendar (5787 / 2026-2027)

Hebrew calendar dates shift each Gregorian year. The dates below are for the **5787** cycle in **Israel** (one yom tov per holiday side, not the diaspora two-day pattern), taken from HebCal with the Israel scheme (`https://www.hebcal.com/?i=on`, API parameter `i=on`) on 26 August 2026. Re-verify before relying on a specific date, and regenerate this table from HebCal with `i=on` when 5788 dates are needed.

### High Holidays / Yamim Noraim (ימים נוראים)

| Holiday | 2026 Gregorian Date | Business Impact |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Leil Selichot | Sep 5, 2026 | No business impact |
| Erev Rosh Hashana | Sep 11, 2026 | Short day (closes early). Falls on a Friday, so the cutoff is early |
| Rosh Hashana (ראש השנה) | Sep 12-13, 2026 | Full shutdown 2 days |
| Erev Yom Kippur | Sep 20, 2026 | Short day (closes early) |
| Yom Kippur (יום כיפור) | Sep 21, 2026 | Complete national shutdown. Even secular Israelis don't work |
| Erev Sukkot | Sep 25, 2026 | Short day (closes early) |
| Sukkot - first day (סוכות) | Sep 26, 2026 | Full shutdown |
| Sukkot - Chol HaMoed | Sep 27 - Oct 2, 2026 | Partial operation; many on vacation. Oct 2 is Hoshana Raba (short day, eve of Shmini Atzeret) |
| Shmini Atzeret / Simchat Torah | Oct 3, 2026 | Full shutdown (single combined day in Israel) |
| Iron Swords Memorial Day (יום הזיכרון לחללי מלחמת חרבות ברזל) | Oct 5, 2026 (24 Tishrei) | National observance. State ceremonies at 11:00 and 13:00. Avoid entertainment events. Set by a government decision of 17 March 2024 |
| Yitzhak Rabin Memorial Day | Oct 22, 2026 | Working day. Ceremonies and school observances; avoid entertainment scheduling |

**Planning note:** The Tishrei cluster (Rosh Hashana through Simchat Torah, plus the Iron Swords Memorial Day on 24 Tishrei) effectively shuts down Israeli business for most of September 11 through October 5, 2026. Build this into any project timeline spanning September-November.

## Seasonal Business Patterns

### Q1 (January-March)

**Pace:** Moderate to slow in January, picks up in February-March.

- January: Post-holiday slow start. Many decisions deferred from previous year
- February: Budget approvals typically land; new annual contracts kick in
- March: Purim disrupts one week. Fiscal year-end planning for companies on April-March cycle
- **VAT reminder:** Jan-Feb report due March 15

### Q2 (April-June)

**Pace:** Very disrupted. Pesach dominates April.

- Pesach effectively halves whichever month it falls in (April in 2026, late April 2027). Full shutdown on the first day and on Shvi'i shel Pesach; the five Chol HaMoed days between them run at low productivity, and Isru Chag the day after is a normal workday. Take the year's exact dates from the table above
- Yom HaZikaron and Yom HaAtzmaut fall back-to-back and create a mini-holiday period. Yom Yerushalayim mostly affects Jerusalem. Shavuot is a single-day shutdown in Israel. Exact dates are in the table above
- June: Strong productivity month. Good for closing deals and launching projects
- **Annual tax return season.** Self-employed freelancers and businesses submit annual reports
- **VAT reminder:** Mar-Apr report due May 15; May-Jun due July 15

### Q3 (July-September)

**Pace:** Slow in July-August, then urgent before holidays.

- July-August: Israeli vacation season. Response rates drop significantly
- August: Many businesses at 50-70% capacity. Not a good month to launch anything important
- September: Back-to-school (usually first week of September). Then High Holiday rush begins
- **September alert:** Rosh Hashana typically falls in September. Everything before the holidays becomes urgent; plan accordingly
- **VAT reminder:** Jul-Aug report due Sep 15

### Q4 (October-December)

**Pace:** Very disrupted in October, strong in November-December.

- The Tishrei cluster dominates, and it can land in September, October or both. In 5787 it runs Sep 11 through Oct 5, 2026, so September rather than October is the lost month that year. Always check the table rather than assuming October
- November: Strong productivity month after holidays. Good for year-end pushes
- December: Chanukah falls here but does not close businesses. December 31 has no special meaning in Israel (fiscal year-end varies by company)
- **VAT reminder:** Sep-Oct report due Nov 15; Nov-Dec report due Jan 15

## Key Planning Rules

**For deadlines:** Add 1-2 weeks buffer for anything due in October (Tishrei holidays), the week of Pesach, or August.

**For launches:** Avoid launching products, campaigns, or major projects in the two weeks before Rosh Hashana, the week of Pesach, or August. The audience is distracted or absent.

**For government filings:** File early. The Tax Authority and Bituach Leumi websites often slow down near deadlines.

**For client proposals:** Avoid sending proposals right before major holidays. They will get lost. Send right after holidays when people are catching up.

**For hiring:** Q1 (Jan-Feb) and post-Pesach (late April, May) are the best hiring windows. Avoid making offers right before the Tishrei holidays; decisions get delayed until after.
